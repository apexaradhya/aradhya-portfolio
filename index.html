01 · CAD

Designed before it is built.

Every mechanism is modeled in Onshape and Fusion before a part is cut. Simulation catches problems early, so build time goes into improving the robot instead of fixing it.

Interference detection

Flags parts that collide or overlap in the assembly before anything is built.

Pressure analysis

Center of gravity

Measures the COG of the full robot to keep it stable when lifting, turning, and climbing.

Code that knows where it is.

The robot runs autonomous routines written in C++. Position tracking, closed-loop control, and planned paths make every run repeatable.

Odometry

Tracking wheels and the IMU estimate the robot’s x, y, and heading in real time.

PID control

Proportional, integral, and derivative terms correct error for fast, precise movements.

Path planning

Routines are planned as paths so the robot reaches each target the same way every match.

01 · Fundamentals

How the model sees.

  • An image is a matrix of pixel intensities from 0 to 255. Color adds 3 channels: red, green, blue
  • Each neuron computes a weighted sum of its inputs plus a bias, then a ReLU activation: max(0, x)
  • Softmax turns the final layer into class probabilities that sum to 1
  • Training: run a forward pass, measure loss, then backpropagate and update weights with gradient descent and a set learning rate
02 · Detection

Sliding window.

  • Turns an image classifier into a detector
  • Crops windows across the whole frame and classifies each as car, truck, or background
  • Downside: thousands of crops per frame, which is too slow for real time driving
03 · CNN Training

Accuracy vs. overfitting.

  • Convolution and pooling layers pick out edges and shapes, feeding a 128 neuron hidden layer and 3 outputs
  • Reached 87% accuracy on a CIFAR-10 subset of cars and trucks
  • Training accuracy kept rising while validation flattened out, so the model was memorizing the training set
04 · Transfer Learning

Fixing overfitting.

  • Trained on top of a pretrained expert model instead of starting from random weights
  • Expanded from 3 classes to 80+ with less training time
  • Accuracy rose to 96%, with training and validation curves staying together
05 · YOLO

YOLOv3 on video.

  • Pretrained YOLOv3 (DarkNet backbone, COCO dataset, 80 classes) run in TensorFlow on a GPU
  • Outputs at 3 scales: 13 × 13, 26 × 26, and 52 × 52 grids, each 255 deep (3 anchor boxes × (4 box values + 1 objectness + 80 classes))
  • 9 anchor boxes split across the 3 scales so small and large objects both get caught
  • Wrapped into a video pipeline with OpenCV: about 50 to 70 ms per frame, detecting cars, trucks, buses, people, bicycles, and traffic lights

01 · Synthesis

Making the gels.

  • Picked agar (seaweed) and HEC (cellulose) because they gel in different ways, so a blend could combine both strengths
  • Used citric acid, a non-toxic food acid, as the cross-linker instead of toxic synthetic ones
  • Each batch: 44 g polymer, 11 g citric acid, 500 g water, cast into 50 g discs
  • Cast fresh gels for every trial to keep results independent
02 · Test Design

Testing what matters to a farmer.

  • Designed a soil-retention test on top of the standard open-air gel test
  • Mixed 600 g of gel into 1,800 g of soil per pot
  • Gave gel pots 50 mL per watering vs. 250 mL for the control, a reduced-irrigation scenario
  • Weighed every pot daily for 8 days, across 7 independent trials
03 · Results

44% less water lost.

  • Blend pot lost 340 g vs. 603 g for the control, holding 263 g more water
  • Blend lost 43.8 g/day, half the control’s 87.6 g/day
  • Blend beat agar alone by 93 g and HEC alone by 144 g
  • Every pairwise difference was significant (p < 0.001)
04 · Analysis

Absorption vs. retention.

  • Found a trade-off: HEC absorbed the most water (16.7% vs. 8.0%) but dried fastest
  • Agar held water longest, with a 2.46-day drying half-time vs. 1.93 for HEC
  • Explains why the blend won: agar’s retention plus HEC’s cohesive network
  • Checked consistency across all 7 trials before drawing conclusions
05 · What I Learned

Takeaways.

01
Test the real use case
  • The open-air test alone pointed to HEC
  • The soil test showed the blend was the real winner
02
Replicate before you believe
  • 7 trials and real statistics turned a hunch into a result
  • Variability checks caught noisy runs early
03
